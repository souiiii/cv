Introduction: Image
• An image is a visual representation of something.

• It can depict real objects, people, places, ideas, or abstract patterns,
  and it is made up of visual elements such as colors, shapes, lines,
  textures, etc.

• For example:
   • A photo you take with your phone is an image.
   • A company logo is an image.
   • A medical X-ray is an image.
   • A satellite picture of Earth is an image.

## PDF page 7

Introduction: Image
Digital images generally fall into two main types:
• Raster images consist of pixels and can lose quality when enlarged.

• Vector images are made from mathematical shapes and can be scaled
  to any size without losing quality.

An image is any visual representation of information, whether created
by a camera, drawn by an artist, or generated by a computer.

## PDF page 9

Introduction: Image
• Image: A visual representation or depiction of an
  object, scene, or phenomenon.
  • Digital Image: It is a two-dimensional array of pixels
    (picture elements), where each pixel corresponds to a
    specific location in the image and carries information
    about the color and intensity of that location.

  • A representation of the original image by a discrete set of
    data points

  • Each of these data points is called a picture element,
    commonly referred to as a “pixel”

## PDF page 16

Applications
• Medical Imaging: Techniques like X-rays, MRIs, CT scans, and ultrasounds rely on
  image processing for visualization, diagnosis, and treatment planning. Image
  enhancement, segmentation, and 3D reconstruction help medical professionals
  identify anomalies and make informed decisions.
• Remote Sensing: Satellite and aerial imagery aid in environmental monitoring,
  disaster assessment, and urban planning. Image processing allows the extraction of
  valuable data, such as land cover classification, vegetation health, and changes over
  time.
• Computer Vision: In fields like robotics and automation, computer vision enables
  machines to interpret and understand visual data. Applications include object
  detection, facial recognition, gesture recognition, and autonomous vehicles.
• Entertainment and Gaming: Special effects in movies, video games, and virtual
  reality are heavily reliant on image processing. Techniques like green screen
  compositing, motion capture, and 3D rendering enhance visual experiences.

---

## PDF page 17

Applications
• Biometrics: Image processing plays a crucial role in biometric systems for personal
  identification, such as fingerprint recognition, iris scanning, and voice analysis.
• Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning: Image analysis is integral to training
  and deploying machine learning models, particularly in image classification, object
  detection, and image generation tasks.
• Security and Surveillance: Image processing aids in video surveillance, detecting
  suspicious activities, and identifying intruders through motion detection and facial
  recognition.
• Quality Control: Industries like manufacturing and agriculture use image processing
  for quality assessment and defect detection in products and produce.
• Archaeology and Cultural Heritage: Image processing helps researchers analyze and
  restore old artworks, artifacts, and manuscripts, preserving cultural heritage.

---

## PDF page 18

Applications
• Geographical Information Systems (GIS): Satellite and aerial images are used to
  create maps, monitor land use changes, and manage natural resources.
• Forensics: Image processing assists in criminal investigations by enhancing and
  analyzing surveillance footage, fingerprints, and facial features.
• Medicine and Biology: Microscopic images from biological studies benefit from
  techniques like cell counting, tissue analysis, and tracking of cellular movements.
• Document Analysis: Handwriting recognition, document classification, and text
  extraction from images facilitate digitization and automation in document
  management.
• Meteorology: Image processing is used to analyze and interpret satellite and radar
  images for weather forecasting and climate research.
• These applications highlight the immense versatility of digital image processing,
  making it an essential tool across various industries, sciences, and technologies.
